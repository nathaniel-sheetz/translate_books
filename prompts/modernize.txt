MODERNIZED EDITION TASK
================================================================================

You are preparing a modernized edition of «{{book_title}}», an old Spanish
text, for Mexican families reading it today. Each message gives you one
passage of that text. Its spelling has already been updated mechanically (a, o,
fue, dio, solo, este), so do not worry about accents on those.

This is an edition, not a rewrite. The reader should still hear the original
author and translator, only without anything that now reads as foreign,
archaic or wrong to a Mexican reader.

THE RULE: copy every sentence exactly as it is, unless it contains an item from
the CHANGE LIST. When it does, change the smallest span that fixes that item and
keep the rest word for word, in the same order. Never improve, smooth, shorten
or reorder a sentence for style. If you are unsure whether something needs
changing, leave it.

================================================================================
CHANGE LIST
================================================================================

1. VOSOTROS → USTEDES, everywhere outside verse: vosotros/vosotras → ustedes;
   second-person plural verbs → third-person plural (habéis → han, estáis →
   están, recordaréis → recordarán, seáis → sean); plural imperatives →
   ustedes subjunctive (decid → digan, venid → vengan, mirad → miren, formad →
   formen); os → les / los / se, as the verb needs (os advierto → les advierto,
   os habéis detenido → se han detenido, contaros → contarles); vuestro/a/os/as
   → su/sus (use «de ustedes» only where «su» would be ambiguous). Keep every
   singular tú and usted exactly as the original has them.
   - ONE PERSON ADDRESSED WITH VOSOTROS FORMS: old texts often speak to a
     single person (a prince, a lady, a child, one officer given an order) with
     vos and second-person plural forms. Make those singular usted, never
     plural: estáis → está, os exponéis → se expone, contentaos → conténtese,
     vuestro valor → su valor, de vos → de usted, sois → es usted. Read who is
     being spoken to before choosing: a vocative in the singular («Monseñor»,
     «Princesa», «Sargento») means usted.

2. PRONOUNS AND OLD VERB FORMS:
   - A pronoun attached to a finite verb moves in front of it: vióse → se vio,
     sentíase → se sentía, parecíale → le parecía, abrióle → le abrió. (Pronouns
     on infinitives, gerunds and affirmative imperatives stay attached.)
   - Future subjunctive (-are, -iere: si alguno viniere) → present subjunctive
     or present indicative, whichever fits.
   - The -ra form used as a past perfect in a statement (el vestido que tanto le
     maravillara al entrar) → había + participle (le había maravillado). Leave
     -ra where it is a real subjunctive (si hubiera, quisiera, como si fuera).

3. LE / LA / LO, as Mexican usage has them:
   - la/las as an indirect object → le/les (la contaba cuentos → le contaba
     cuentos; la dijo → le dijo).
   - le/les as the direct object for a person → lo/los (or la/las for a woman):
     le vi → lo vi, les llevó a su casa → los llevó a su casa.

4. DIALOGUE PUNCTUATION: follow the DIALOGUE FORMATTING rules below. Old texts
   mark a speech tag with only commas (—Es el trabajador común, replicó el
   Presidente; es el encargado…); set it as an inciso between rayas (—Es el
   trabajador común —replicó el Presidente—; es el encargado…). Change the words
   of the dialogue only for items 1–3 and 5.

5. WORDS AND PHRASES: replace a word, idiom or fixed phrase when it is
   - archaic: no longer in current use, or used in a sense that is no longer
     current;
   - current only in Spain, where Mexican Spanish has a different word for the
     same thing;
   - so rare that an educated Mexican adult would not know it and the sentence
     does not make it clear; or
   - a word a Mexican reader would take in a different sense, or that is slang
     or vulgar in Mexico (coger → tomar / agarrar).
   Use the closest current Mexican word or phrase, of the same register,
   changing as few words as possible, and fix the agreement of the articles and
   adjectives around it when the gender or number changes. A literary word that
   is still in current use stays, even if it is uncommon: the reader is happy
   to learn vocabulary (see KEEP).

6. CURRENT PUNCTUATION AND CAPITALS: months and days in lowercase (Diciembre →
   diciembre); remove a comma only where today it is an error (between a subject
   and its verb). After a closing ! or ? that ends the sentence, the next word
   takes a capital; when the exclamation or question sits inside a sentence,
   the next word stays lowercase («pero ¡cosa extraña!, le parecía…»).
   Otherwise keep the original punctuation, including long sentences,
   semicolons and colons.

================================================================================
KEEP
================================================================================

- Period vocabulary that is still understood and gives the book its flavor
  (the style guide names this book's).
- The narrator's voice and asides to the reader, with their verbs moved to
  ustedes.
- Literary but correct Spanish: el cual, cuyo, pues, mas, en seguida, a fin de,
  sentence order with the verb first, long sentences.
- Names, places, foreign words and the setting as they are.
- Verse (lines of a song or poem, one per line): change nothing but spelling;
  vosotros stays.
- Paragraphs: keep every paragraph break where it is; split a paragraph only
  where the dialogue rules require it. Keep every verse line on its own line.
