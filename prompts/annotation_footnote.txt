## What a good gloss is

- **One or two sentences.** Thirty words is a good ceiling. An endnote that runs
  long stops being a gloss.
- **Concrete.** Say what the thing is, where or when it is, and why it matters
  here. "Ciudad de la región de Aragón, en el noreste de España" beats "un lugar
  en España".
- **Pitched at the book's reader.** Match the register in the style guide. A
  children's book gets a plainer gloss than a scientific one.
- **Self-contained.** The reader has the sentence in front of them, not the
  annotation. Never write "aquí significa…" or refer to "the marked word".
- **No hedging about facts.** If you are not confident of a fact, leave it out
  rather than qualifying it. Do not invent dates, populations, or etymologies.
  Charity toward the author ("parece que quiso decir") is not hedging: it is
  about him, not about the fact.

Do NOT include the bracketed anchor in `note_text` — it is re-attached
automatically. Do NOT prefix the gloss with any marker or attribution. Write only
the gloss text itself.

If the annotation names a person, place, historical event, unit of measure,
foreign phrase, literary allusion, or a term of art, gloss that. If the source
sentence itself already explains the thing, say so via `state`.

## Two kinds of note, one voice

A footnote does one of two jobs.

- **Explanatory.** The author mentions something in passing that a modern reader
  may not know: a place, a coin, a person, a custom, a date that was "two hundred
  years ago" when he wrote it. Say what it is. Nothing in the text is wrong.
- **Corrective.** The author said something that is not so: a belief of his day
  that later knowledge revised, or an honest slip in a unit, a date or a figure.
  Say what is so.

Write both as a friend of the author would. The reader trusts and likes the
author, and the note is on his side: it adds to what he said, it never scores a
point against him. An error is an honest mistake, made in good faith, and often
what everyone believed at the time.

- **Kind is about stance, not softness.** Short and direct is fine, and most
  explanatory notes should be exactly that. Do not pad a note with reassurance.
- **Start from what the author had right**, when there is something: the belief
  of his time, the case where his figure holds, what he evidently meant. Lead
  with that rather than with the contradiction.
- **Make clear which to believe.** Kind is not vague. A note that states the
  modern fact without acknowledging what the sentence says leaves the reader
  guessing which of the two is true.
- **Name the speaker the reader knows**: the narrator or character who says it
  («tío Paul»), or «el autor» when the book has no such figure.
- **Never write against the author.** No note whose whole point is that he was
  wrong (a bare «X no: Y» or «No X, sino Y»), no «error», «se equivoca», «falso»
  or «erróneamente», no irony, no exclamations.

If the book's own published notes are in front of you, match how they name the
speaker and how long they run. They never license a note against the author.

Examples from books in this series. Match their manner, not their words.

Explanatory:

- "Según las sagas nórdicas, un guerrero que luchaba con una furia
  descontrolada, aparentemente sin sentir dolor ni miedo." — on *berserker*.
- "El 15 de julio. Quintilis era el antiguo nombre de julio." — on *los idus de
  Quintilis*.
- "Esa erupción del Etna fue en 1669: «hace doscientos años» cuando el tío Paul
  se lo contaba a los niños." — a date that was right when the book was written
  is explained, not corrected.

Corrective:

- "Eso es cierto de alguien haciendo ejercicio fuerte. En reposo se necesitan
  unos cuatrocientos litros de aire por hora." — the author gave six thousand
  litres an hour. It replaced a draft that answered «…no seis mil».
- "Tío Paul no está considerando aquí las piñas del pino, ni ciertas clases de
  plantas que se reproducen por medio de esporas, como los helechos y los
  musgos." — on «toda planta tiene su flor». It replaced «El pino no: tiene
  piñas».
- "Parece que quiso decir toneladas, no kilogramos: un cubo de cobre de un
  kilómetro de lado pesa unos nueve mil millones de toneladas." — an honest slip,
  read as what he meant.
- "En tiempos antiguos se creía que algunos elefantes y ballenas podían vivir
  cientos de años. De hecho los elefantes viven varias décadas y algunas ballenas
  pueden superar los 200 años." — a belief of the time, given as one.
- "Tío Paul aquí las tiene al revés: las obreras empiezan de nodrizas en casa;
  más tarde hacen cera, y ya mayores salen a recolectar." — the first draft gave
  only the right order, never saying the sentence had it backwards, and the
  reader could not tell which to believe.

## Classify the annotation first

Set `state` to:

- `"needs_help"` — the note is blank, contains only the bracketed anchor, or
  contains a short instruction naming what to gloss (e.g. `biblia`, `comillas`,
  `poesía`). This is the normal case: write the gloss.
- `"already_resolved"` — the note already contains a written gloss. Set
  `recommendation` to a one-line English restatement and leave `note_text` empty.
  Do not rewrite or improve an existing gloss.

The line between the two is whether the note **says something about** the marked
span or merely **names a subject**.

An instruction names a subject and stops: `biblia` means "gloss this Bible
reference", not "the gloss is the word biblia". Same for `comillas`, `poesía`.

A gloss says something, and it does **not** have to be long. All of these are
finished notes — return `already_resolved` and leave them alone:

- `[No-Name] «Pequeño sin nombre»` — translates the marked name.
- `[Meadow»] Prado de Misty` — identifies what the name refers to.
- `Himno cristiano por George Washington Doane (1848)` — identifies the allusion.
  (A gloss need not carry a bracket at all; the anchor is optional.)

A short gloss is not an unfinished one. Do not "improve" a brief note into a
longer one — the reader wrote what they wanted the book to say.

## Output

Respond with ONLY a JSON object, no other text, in exactly this shape:

{
  "key": "<echo the key given below, verbatim>",
  "state": "needs_help" | "already_resolved",
  "state_reason": "<one short clause in English explaining the state choice>",
  "recommendation": "<one sentence in {{target_language}} describing what you glossed and why — this goes in the report, not the book>",
  "note_text": "<the published gloss, in {{target_language}}, 1-2 sentences, no brackets, no marker; empty string when state is already_resolved>",
  "confidence": "high" | "medium" | "low",
  "evidence": ["<short strings naming what the gloss rests on>"]
}

Set `confidence` to `"low"` when the gloss depends on a fact you are unsure of.
A low-confidence gloss is still reported, and the reader decides.
