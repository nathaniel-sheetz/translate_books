HOW TO CHECK FORMS OF ADDRESS (usted vs. tú)

You are checking whether characters address each other with the expected register
(formal usted vs. informal tú) according to the book's ADDRESS MAP. This is about
second-person address in dialogue, not narration.

IDENTIFYING THE FORM IN SPANISH
- tú (informal): the pronoun tú/ti/contigo; object/reflexive te; possessive
  tu/tus; 2nd-person-singular verb endings (tienes, quieres, dijiste, ven, dime,
  ¿cómo estás?).
- usted (formal): the pronoun usted; object lo/la/le/se; possessive su/sus used
  for the addressee; 3rd-person-singular verb endings directed at the listener
  (tiene, quiere, dijo, venga, dígame, ¿cómo está?).
- Judge the form actually used for the ADDRESSEE. su/le are ambiguous out of
  context (they also mean his/her/their) — only treat them as usted when the
  sentence is clearly addressed to the listener.

ATTRIBUTING A LINE
- Determine who is SPEAKING and who is being ADDRESSED (the direction), using the
  speech attributions and surrounding narration. If you cannot tell who is being
  addressed, do NOT flag it — mark it ambiguous at most.

INFERRING THE SITUATION
- Some expectations depend on the scene being PUBLIC vs. PRIVATE. Infer this from
  the surrounding source narration: are third parties present? is it a formal or
  ceremonial setting? Use the address map's per-situation rules accordingly.

APPLYING THE MAP (per addressed line)
1. Identify speaker → addressee.
2. Infer the situation (public / private / other).
3. Find that pair in the address map. Each DIRECTION is independent (A→B may
   differ from B→A). Within the direction, apply the FIRST rule whose `when`
   condition and chapter window (`since`/`until`/`after_event`) match the current
   chapter; otherwise the `when: "default"` rule.
4. If no pair is listed, fall back to the map's GLOBAL RULES.
5. Compare the form actually used against the expected form.

OUT OF SCOPE (do not flag)
- voseo (vos / tenés / querés) and plural address (ustedes / vosotros) — v1 only
  checks the tú-vs-usted singular choice.
- Narration, quoted thoughts about a third party, and lines whose addressee you
  cannot determine.

Every finding must record the resolved direction and situation, e.g.
"public setting, Astrida→Ricardo: expected usted, found tú".
