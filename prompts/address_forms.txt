HOW TO CHECK FORMS OF ADDRESS (usted vs. tú)

You are checking whether characters address each other with the expected register
(formal usted vs. informal tú) according to the book's ADDRESS MAP. This is about
second-person address in dialogue. Narration is out of scope UNLESS the address
map brings a narrating voice into scope (for example a teacher's voice that
speaks to the pupil directly). Such a voice is checked exactly like a quoted
speaker, even though its lines carry no quotation marks or rayas: every
unquoted command, question or possessive aimed at the reader is an addressed line.

IDENTIFYING THE FORM IN SPANISH
- tú (informal): the pronoun tú/ti/contigo; object/reflexive te; possessive
  tu/tus; 2nd-person-singular verb endings (tienes, quieres, dijiste, ven, dime,
  ¿cómo estás?).
- usted (formal): the pronoun usted; object lo/la/le/se; possessive su/sus used
  for the addressee; 3rd-person-singular verb endings directed at the listener
  (tiene, quiere, dijo, venga, dígame, ¿cómo está?).
- Judge the form actually used for the ADDRESSEE. su/le are ambiguous out of
  context (they also mean his/her/their) — only treat them as usted when the
  sentence is clearly addressed to the listener.

ATTRIBUTING A LINE
- Determine who is SPEAKING and who is being ADDRESSED (the direction), using the
  speech attributions and surrounding narration. If you cannot tell who is being
  addressed, do NOT flag it — mark it ambiguous at most.

INFERRING THE SITUATION
- Some expectations depend on the scene being PUBLIC vs. PRIVATE. Infer this from
  the surrounding source narration: are third parties present? is it a formal or
  ceremonial setting? Use the address map's per-situation rules accordingly.

APPLYING THE MAP (per addressed line)
1. Identify speaker → addressee.
2. Infer the situation (public / private / other).
3. Find that pair in the address map. Each DIRECTION is independent (A→B may
   differ from B→A). Within the direction, apply the FIRST rule whose `when`
   condition and chapter window (`since`/`until`/`after_event`) match the current
   chapter; otherwise the `when: "default"` rule.
4. If no pair is listed, fall back to the map's GLOBAL RULES.
5. Compare the form actually used against the expected form.

NUMBER (singular vs. plural address) — ONLY WHEN THE MAP SAYS "NUMBER IS CHECKED"
If the address map contains the words NUMBER IS CHECKED, the singular/plural
choice is in scope for the pairs that section names, and a mismatch is a
violation (rule "wrong-number"). For a pair the map says is addressed in the
singular, any of these aimed at the addressee is plural drift:
- the pronoun ustedes / vosotros, incl. "todos ustedes", "por ustedes mismos",
  "detrás de ustedes";
- 3rd-person-plural verb endings directed at the listener: plural commands
  (miren, piensen, presten, lleven, observen, cuenten) and plural statements or
  questions (ustedes saben, deben, ¿han visto?);
- object/reflexive les / los / se and possessive su / sus standing for the
  addressees ("les diré", "sus pizarras");
- a plural object where the map says each addressee has one ("tus pizarras"
  for "your slates" when the map wants "tu pizarra").
Rewrite to the singular form the map expects (Sabes…, Piensa…, tu pizarra).
For a pair the map says is addressed in the plural, the reverse applies.
NOT plural address: a 3rd-person plural about OTHER people ("que los alumnos
observen", "los niños vean"), lines that speak to someone the number rule does
not cover (instructions to the teacher, a character addressing a group the map
licenses), and a plural object that is genuinely plural ("tus manos").
Without the words NUMBER IS CHECKED in the map, number is out of scope.

OUT OF SCOPE (do not flag)
- voseo (vos / tenés / querés).
- plural address (ustedes / vosotros), unless the NUMBER section above applies.
- Narration the map does not bring into scope, quoted thoughts about a third
  party, and lines whose addressee you cannot determine.

Every finding must record the resolved direction and situation, e.g.
"public setting, Astrida→Ricardo: expected usted, found tú" or "teacher→pupil:
expected singular tú, found plural ustedes".
